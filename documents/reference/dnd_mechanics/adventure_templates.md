# D&D Adventure Templates and Quest Hooks

## The Five-Room Dungeon

A classic structure for one-session adventures (2-4 hours).

### Room 1: Entrance and Guardian

**Purpose**: Introduce setting, establish stakes
**Contents**:

- Guardian or obstacle that hints at what's inside
- Clue about the theme or villain
- Decision point: Press on or turn back?

**Examples**:

- Corrupted guardian offering cryptic warning
- Locked door requiring puzzle solution
- Friendly NPC with information (and ulterior motive)

### Room 2: Puzzle or Roleplaying Challenge

**Purpose**: Change pace, reward non-combat skills
**Contents**:

- Environmental puzzle (pressure plates, riddles, ciphers)
- Social encounter (negotiate with trapped creatures)
- Exploration challenge (navigate maze, find hidden passage)

**Examples**:

- Trapped room requiring coordination to escape
- NPC who needs convincing to reveal secret
- Ancient mechanism requiring knowledge check to operate

### Room 3: Setback or Twist

**Purpose**: Raise tension, complicate situation
**Contents**:

- False victory followed by real threat
- Environmental hazard (collapsing ceiling, flooding)
- Betrayal or unexpected enemy

**Examples**:

- Treasure is trapped, alerts enemies
- Rescued NPC is actually polymorphed villain
- Path forward is blocked, must find alternate route

### Room 4: Climax Battle

**Purpose**: Major combat encounter
**Contents**:

- Boss fight or significant enemy group
- Environmental factors (cover, hazards, elevation)
- Minions or waves of enemies

**Examples**:

- Villain on elevated platform with ranged support
- Beast in lair with difficult terrain
- Multiple enemies with tactical advantages

### Room 5: Reward and Revelation

**Purpose**: Resolution, payoff, hook for future
**Contents**:

- Treasure appropriate to challenge
- Information revealing larger plot
- NPC who provides next quest

**Examples**:

- Villain's journal revealing conspiracy
- Artifact that villain was seeking
- Portal to next adventure location

## Quest Hook Templates

### The Desperate Plea

**Structure**: NPC needs immediate help
**Variants**:

- "My child was kidnapped by goblins!"
- "The village is under attack!"
- "I've been poisoned, only [item] can cure me!"

**Development**:

- Real problem + hidden complication
- Example: Kidnapped child is actually runaway, goblins protecting them from abusive parent

### The Mysterious Benefactor

**Structure**: Powerful patron offers lucrative job
**Variants**:

- Anonymous letter with payment and instructions
- Hooded figure in tavern with proposition
- Royal summons with vague details

**Development**:

- Patron has ulterior motive
- Job is more dangerous than described
- Patron is testing party for larger role

### The Personal Stakes

**Structure**: Ties to character backstory
**Variants**:

- Relative arrives with news from home
- Organization from character's past makes contact
- Enemy from backstory resurfaces

**Development**:

- Resolving quest changes character's trajectory
- Forces choice between party loyalty and personal goals
- Reveals truth about character's past

### The Greater Good

**Structure**: Threat to innocent people
**Variants**:

- Disease spreading through city
- Natural disaster looming
- Tyrannical ruler oppressing region

**Development**:

- Solution requires sacrifice or difficult choice
- Solving one problem creates another
- Innocent people have different ideas about "help"

### The Mystery

**Structure**: Strange occurrence needs investigation
**Variants**:

- Bodies found with no explanation
- Magical phenomenon causing chaos
- Ancient ruins suddenly appear

**Development**:

- Initial theory is wrong
- Investigation uncovers conspiracy
- Party becomes target during investigation

## Adventure Structures

### The Heist

**Acts**:

1. **Planning**: Gather information, recruit experts
2. **Infiltration**: Get inside target location
3. **Execution**: Steal item, bypass security
4. **Escape**: Get out before caught
5. **Fallout**: Deal with consequences

**Key Elements**:

- Multiple approaches possible
- Stealth and social skills important
- Time pressure increases tension

### The Murder Mystery

**Acts**:

1. **Discovery**: Body found, scene examined
2. **Investigation**: Interview witnesses, gather clues
3. **Red Herrings**: False leads complicate case
4. **Revelation**: True culprit revealed
5. **Confrontation**: Accuse and apprehend killer

**Key Elements**:

- Every NPC has motive and alibi
- Physical evidence can be misleading
- Killer may try to kill again

### The Rescue Mission

**Acts**:

1. **Abduction**: NPC captured by enemies
2. **Tracking**: Follow trail to enemy location
3. **Infiltration**: Enter enemy stronghold
4. **Extraction**: Free captive and escape
5. **Return**: Bring captive home safely

**Key Elements**:

- Time limit before captive is moved/killed
- Captive may be injured or unwilling to leave
- Enemy pursuit during escape

### The Wilderness Trek

**Acts**:

1. **Preparation**: Gather supplies, hire guide
2. **Journey**: Navigate hazardous terrain
3. **Encounters**: Random events and obstacles
4. **Destination**: Reach goal location
5. **Return**: Deal with changes during absence

**Key Elements**:

- Resource management (food, water, supplies)
- Random encounters with wilderness creatures
- Environmental challenges (weather, terrain)

### The Political Intrigue

**Acts**:

1. **Introduction**: Enter political landscape
2. **Alliances**: Choose factions to support
3. **Maneuvering**: Social encounters and deals
4. **Crisis**: Threat to political stability
5. **Resolution**: Reshape political order

**Key Elements**:

- Multiple factions with competing goals
- Social skills and negotiation crucial
- Betrayal and shifting alliances

## Scene Types

### Combat Encounter

**Purpose**: Test tactical skills, resource management
**Elements**:

- Clear objective (defeat enemies, hold position, escape)
- Interesting terrain (cover, elevation, hazards)
- Enemy tactics (coordinated, defensive, reckless)

### Social Encounter

**Purpose**: Roleplay, gather information, influence NPCs
**Elements**:

- NPC motivation (what do they want?)
- Stakes (what happens if party fails?)
- Multiple approaches (persuasion, intimidation, deception)

### Exploration Encounter

**Purpose**: Discover secrets, navigate environment
**Elements**:

- Hidden information or items
- Environmental hazards
- Multiple paths or solutions

### Puzzle Encounter

**Purpose**: Problem-solving, reward player creativity
**Elements**:

- Clear goal (open door, disable trap, decipher code)
- Multiple clues or approaches
- Consequence for failure (not instant death!)

## Adventure Pacing

### The Three-Act Structure

**Act 1: Setup (25% of session)**

- Introduce quest and NPCs
- Establish stakes
- Give party direction
- First minor conflict

**Act 2: Complications (50% of session)**

- Main challenges and encounters
- Setbacks and revelations
- Resource depletion
- Rising tension

**Act 3: Climax (25% of session)**

- Final confrontation
- Resolution of main plot
- Reward and consequences
- Hook for next adventure

## Campaign Arcs

### The Building Threat

**Structure**:

- **Tier 1 (Levels 1-4)**: Hear rumors of villain
- **Tier 2 (Levels 5-10)**: Encounter villain's minions
- **Tier 3 (Levels 11-16)**: Direct conflict with villain
- **Tier 4 (Levels 17-20)**: Final confrontation

**Development**:

- Each adventure reveals more about villain
- Villain's power grows with party's level
- Stakes escalate from local to world-ending

### The Mystery Box

**Structure**:

- Present mysterious artifact/phenomenon
- Each adventure reveals one clue
- Party slowly pieces together truth
- Final revelation changes everything

**Development**:

- Early adventures seem unconnected
- Pattern emerges around level 5-7
- Truth is different than initial theory

### The Faction War

**Structure**:

- Multiple factions with competing goals
- Party's choices determine faction strength
- Alliances shift based on party actions
- Endgame determined by faction balance

**Development**:

- No faction is purely good/evil
- Every choice has consequences
- Party's reputation affects NPC reactions

## Adventure Complications

### Time Pressure

- "Ritual completes in 3 days"
- "Plague spreads each day"
- "Army arrives in 1 week"

### Resource Scarcity

- "No rest available in dungeon"
- "Can't buy supplies in hostile town"
- "Spell components hard to find"

### Moral Dilemma

- "Save town or pursue villain?"
- "Sacrifice one to save many?"
- "Follow orders or do what's right?"

### Unreliable Information

- "Map is outdated/incorrect"
- "NPC lied about danger level"
- "Quest giver has hidden agenda"

## Random Adventure Seeds

1. **The Stolen Heirloom**: Noble's family artifact taken by thieves, but artifact contains trapped fiend
2. **The Plague Ship**: Ghost ship arrives in harbor, crew infected with magical disease
3. **The Talking Sword**: Sentient weapon seeks hero, but has hidden agenda
4. **The False Idol**: New cult leader is charlatan, but real deity takes notice
5. **The Forgotten Mine**: Miners uncover ancient city, awakening its guardians
6. **The Cursed Forest**: Trees come alive at night, trapping travelers
7. **The Perfect Crime**: Master thief frames party for heist they didn't commit
8. **The Last Heir**: Young noble is target of assassination, party must protect
9. **The Broken Portal**: Planar rift spills creatures into world, must close it
10. **The Betrayed Dragon**: Dragon seeks revenge on former ally, party caught in middle

## Session Zero Template

### Topics to Cover

1. **Campaign Tone**: Gritty vs heroic, serious vs comedic
2. **House Rules**: Modifications to RAW
3. **Character Connections**: How party knows each other
4. **Safety Tools**: X-card, lines and veils
5. **Schedule**: When/how often to play
6. **Expectations**: Player and DM responsibilities

### Character Creation Together

- Ensure party balance (roles covered)
- Build relationships between PCs
- Tie characters to campaign setting
- Establish party's reputation and goals
